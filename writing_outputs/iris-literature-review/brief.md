# Review brief

Question: Which design and evaluation considerations follow from existing evidence when a low-cost companion displays an animated face while processing speech?

Audience: Human-robot interaction researchers, engineering students, and designers of small display-based companions.

Scope/date range: Purposefully selected sources published from 2009 through 2026, revisited on 13 October 2026. Historical sources establish terminology and mechanisms; recent work supplies adjacent architectures and review context. This is a focused narrative review, not an exhaustive or systematic review.

Inclusion criteria: Social-gaze definitions, facial-display perception, conversation-related gaze and feedback, timestamped-update freshness, and affordable deployment architectures. Retain empirical, conceptual, review, and theoretical work with explicit roles. Inspect repositories separately as practical prior art.

Exclusion criteria: Publication logistics, student anecdotes, clinical effectiveness without relevant evidence, and eye-guided industrial manipulation without a visual-communication role. These materials in the supplied dossier informed scope and caution but were not forced into the technical bibliography.

Length/style: A complete engineering-journal manuscript in standalone IEEEtran journal LaTeX, with an abstract, keywords, methods, thematic synthesis, comparison tables, research gaps, limitations, conclusion, disclosure, and numbered references.

Authors: Eklavya Nathani, Manit Bisht, Anis Fatema, in the order supplied by the user. Institutional affiliation intentionally omitted at the user's request.

Assumptions: No destination journal was specified. IEEEtran provides a standard engineering-journal layout without claiming acceptance or compliance with a particular journal's article-type and submission rules. No new hardware or participant experiments are authorized or needed for this review.

Research agenda: Explicitly identify gaps in the inspected corpus and how further work would inform design, rather than asserting that no earlier work exists.

Skills applied: literature-review-lite, literature-review-from-pdfs, unslop, and unslop-pre2019 from the project's skills directory. All Ponytail files were read; their stated scope is code, not prose, and they are not used to shorten away scientific content. Existing artifacts were preserved.
