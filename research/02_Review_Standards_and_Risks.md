# Literature-review standards, AI use and reputation risks

Research summary updated 13 October 2026. Policies are examples from named publishers, not universal rules or a guarantee of acceptance.

## Can AI help produce a legitimate review?

Yes. AI can assist discovery, comparison, organization and drafting. Legitimacy depends on the resulting scholarship and the selected venue's policies: real references, accurately represented findings, a useful synthesis, transparent methods and authors who can take responsibility for the text.

Rewriting generated prose improves neither a fabricated citation nor an unsupported conclusion. Human revision should include reading the relevant source passages, correcting comparisons and understanding the argument. There is no reliable assurance that a wholly unchecked generated manuscript will be publishable or consequence-free.

## What a credible focused review needs

| Requirement | Practical interpretation |
|---|---|
| A bounded question | Address a specific design problem rather than summarizing all AI or robotics. |
| A justified contribution | Explain what the comparison helps a reader understand beyond individual paper summaries. |
| Honest selection methods | State where, when and how sources were found; explain selection and access limits. |
| Accurate evidence | Check sample sizes, tasks, representations, outcomes and the authors' limitations. |
| Synthesis | Compare agreements, differences and transfer limits across sources. |
| Proportionate conclusions | Distinguish demonstrated findings, design interpretations and future hypotheses. |
| Complete references | Match authors, titles, publication versions and identifiers; identify preprints honestly. |

[SANRA](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41073-019-0064-8) is a published appraisal scale covering review importance, aims, search description, referencing, scientific reasoning and relevant endpoint information. It was developed in medical publishing. It is a useful prompt for checking a narrative review, **not a robotics reporting standard, universal acceptance cutoff or certificate of publishability**.

A narrative review may use purposive selection. Calling the work a systematic or scoping review would create additional methodological expectations that the searches performed here do not meet. Do not invent database searches, screening counts, independent reviewers, quality scores or experiments. There is no universal minimum citation count that makes a review credible; the earlier 12-18-reference suggestion was a workload estimate.

## AI policies actually checked

| Venue/publisher | Policy relevant to this task |
|---|---|
| [Reinvention](https://reinventionjournal.org/index.php/reinvention/AIandAuthorship) | AI cannot be an author. Use in any manuscript element must be disclosed, including what, how and where it was used. Human authors remain responsible. Noncompliance can lead to removal from consideration or retraction. |
| [IEEE publications](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/) | Generated text, figures, images and code require acknowledgment identifying the system, affected sections and extent of use. Grammar/editing assistance has a narrower exception; checking generated output is advised. |

The college's informal attitude does not replace the destination's rules. Policies change and differ between publishers and venues. These examples establish that AI assistance is not universally prohibited; they do not authorize hiding substantial drafting wherever disclosure is required. A statement describing substantial drafting as only grammar correction would be inaccurate.

## Rejection versus blacklisting

Ordinary rejection for scope, insufficient contribution, weak methods or presentation is an editorial outcome. [Elsevier's editorial guidance](https://www.elsevier.com/connect/why-on-earth-did-you-reject-my-paper) explicitly describes these kinds of rejection reasons.

Publisher sanctions are a different process. [ACM's policy overview](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/toc) describes penalties for proven publication-policy violations, including plagiarism, misrepresentation, falsification and misconduct in peer review. Depending on the violation, sanctions can include retraction or bans on future submissions. The relevant overview was available through indexed official policy text; direct retrieval was blocked during this check.

**The researched policies do not support the fear that an honest, mediocre manuscript is automatically blacklisted merely because it is rejected.** This is a bounded inference from the policies checked, not a promise about every outlet or every possible action. Invented evidence, plagiarism, false authorship or failure to follow required disclosure creates a materially different risk. Submit a sincere, coherent review rather than deliberately trying to trigger rejection.

## Google Scholar exposure

[Google Scholar's inclusion guidance](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/inclusion.html) concerns discoverable scholarly web material, including preprints and reports as well as journal articles. Public accessibility, rather than journal acceptance alone, is relevant.

- An ordinary private submission does not itself create publicly crawlable content. This is an inference from the inclusion requirements, conditional on the outlet keeping submissions private.
- Rejection does not itself create a public paper.
- A public preprint or repository upload can be indexed before peer review. Check the submission system for any preprint-posting option or term.
- A weak public publication could affect someone's judgment of your work. That is context-dependent; it is not an automatic Scholar penalty established by the documentation.
- Removing an item from a profile is not removal of the publication from the underlying search index. [Scholar profile guidance](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/citations.html).

For your stated submission-focused objective, public posting is unnecessary unless the teacher specifically requires it. An authentic private submission is the more relevant route to investigate.
